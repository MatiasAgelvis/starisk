I built a second rating for Coursera courses using only the reviews learners marked helpful.

Browse all the scores: https://starisk.matiasagelvis.com

Coursera's headline average includes every star click. I wanted the version shaped by the reviews people actually found useful, so I pulled up to 20 per course, 15,895 reviews total across 1,287 courses (about 12 per course on average). I weighted each review by the square root of (1 + upvotes) and compared that with the headline.

Why trust me: I completed 72 Coursera credentials over about four years, 61 courses and 11 programs, many through financial aid. I built this out of my own experience choosing courses. I’d taken a 4.9 course that was bad, a 4.7 course that was good, and a 4.8 course that was excellent. The headline rating wasn’t separating them. The upvoted reviews were.

As of 25 November, I crawled 1,669 courses and scored 1,287 of them; the rest had no readable reviews. Of the 1,287 scored, 1,047 (81%) have both a headline rating and a helpful-review score. All percentages below use that 1,047:

- The median score is 0.20 stars below the headline.
- 217 (21%, about one in five) sit a full star or more below.
- 113 (11%, about one in nine) sit 1.5 or more below.
- The widest gap is 3.4 stars: a course rated 4.4 whose helpful reviews are mostly one and two stars.

A few caveats, in order of importance:

- These are the reviews people voted helpful, so they skew toward strong opinions. The score reflects what those reviews say, not a formal quality grade.
- Courses with fewer than 15 readable reviews carry a lowSample flag. Treat those as a hint, not a verdict.
- 71% of the reviews that went in are five-star, so this is not just a pile of complaints.
